The Ministry of Wildlife Conservation and Tourism is dedicated to safeguarding Africa's largest intact wilderness corridors and fostering economic opportunities through authentic cultural & natural heritage.

Our Organization

Protecting South Sudan’s Natural Legacy

The Ministry of Wildlife Conservation and Tourism serves as the primary government authority responsible for the stewardship, policy formulation, and legal protection of South Sudan's vast biological diversity, national parks, and cultural heritage sites.

Across more than 200,000 km² of protected wilderness, our ministry oversees critical conservation ecosystems including the Boma-Badingilo landscape and the world's largest wetland—The Sudd. We oversee the protection of the Great Nile Migration, where over 6 million white-eared kob, tiang, and gazelles travel seasonally.

Through proactive ranger patrols, anti-poaching frameworks, community-based conservation initiatives, and international partnerships, we ensure that South Sudan’s natural treasures remain resilient while creating transformative eco-tourism avenues for local communities.

Guiding Principles

Vision, Mission & Core Pillars

Building a resilient eco-tourism economy grounded in integrity, conservation, and community benefit.

Our Vision

To establish South Sudan as a premiere global destination for sustainable eco-tourism and wildlife research, recognized worldwide for pristine national parks, thriving animal migrations, and vibrant living cultural kingdoms.

Our Mission

To safeguard and sustainably manage South Sudan’s wildlife resources, wetland ecosystems, and cultural sites through stringent conservation frameworks, community empowerment, and structured tourism development.

Core Values

Environmental Stewardship, Transparency, Community Integration, Cultural Respect, and Sustainable Infrastructure Development across all 10 States and Administrative Areas.
